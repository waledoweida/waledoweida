How we build a professional website, step by step

By Waled Abu Oweida
October 11, 2026 · 6 min read

Many people think building a website means picking a nice look, putting it online and calling it done. In reality, a website that actually brings in customers is built in clear stages, and each stage shapes the next. In this article I'll walk you through the process I've refined over 9 years, step by step, and explain exactly what a well-built site does for your business.

1. Understanding before designing

The first thing I do isn't opening a design tool; it's listening. We sit down and I ask a few simple but essential questions:

  • What is the site for? Bringing in enquiries? Bookings? Selling online? Or building trust before a customer gets in touch?
  • Who is your customer? Their age, country, what they're looking for and what worries them before they buy.
  • What are your competitors doing? I review their sites and pages so we can stand out rather than copy them.
  • What should a visitor do? Message you on WhatsApp, fill in a form, call, or buy straight away.

These answers drive everything that follows, from the page order to the colour of the buttons.

2. Structure and content

Once the goal is clear, I map out the site: which pages it needs, what goes on each one and how visitors move between them. The aim is that anyone can reach what they want in two or three taps at most.

Then comes the content, the most important part and the one most people underestimate:

  • Copy that sells: not just a description of the service, but words that answer customers' questions and show them what they gain.
  • Real photos: pictures of you, your work and your premises build far more trust than stock images.
  • Arabic and English: if you serve customers in the Gulf or work with international clients, an English version opens a new door.
Tip: Start gathering real photos of your work early. A clear shot of your product or your team makes a big difference to how much visitors trust you.

3. Experience and visual design

This is where the plan takes shape. I always design mobile-first, because most of your visitors arrive on their phones, especially if they come from Facebook, Instagram or TikTok ads.

  • A clear identity: your colours, fonts and logo, so the site feels like yours and not a template shared by countless others.
  • Clear calls to action: every page has one obvious next step, with a button that's easy to spot.
  • A WhatsApp button: in Egypt, Libya and the Gulf, WhatsApp is the easiest way for customers to reach you. A fixed button that opens a chat with a ready-made message removes friction.

4. Development

Good looks aren't enough if the site is slow or buggy. At this stage I focus on:

  • Speed: the site must load quickly, even on mobile data. Visitors won't wait, and Google measures this through what it calls Core Web Vitals.
  • Security: HTTPS (the padlock next to your address) is essential; without it, browsers may warn visitors that the site isn't secure.
  • Clean code: easy to update and extend later without breaking anything.

Together we choose the right approach for your business:

  • A custom build: faster, lighter and tailored exactly to you; ideal for company and service sites.
  • WordPress: a good fit if you'll publish a lot of content or want a familiar dashboard and ready-made plugins.
  • Shopify: a strong choice if your goal is an online store that sells and takes payments.

None of these is always best. The right one is the one that fits your goals and budget.

5. SEO foundations

A site nobody can find is like a shop on a closed street. That's why I set up the SEO basics from day one:

  • A title and description for every page: written with the words your customers actually search for.
  • Structured data: tells Google what your business is and where it is, helping it present your details more clearly.
  • A sitemap: so Google can find all your pages easily.
  • Google Search Console: I connect the site so we can monitor your visibility and catch problems early.
  • Your Google Business Profile: linked to the site to strengthen your presence when people search for your service nearby.

6. Tracking and measurement

If you're going to run ads, you need to know where every pound, dinar or riyal goes. So I install:

  • Google Analytics 4: shows where visitors come from and what they do on the site.
  • Meta and TikTok pixels: so the platforms learn who engaged and show your ads to the people most likely to buy.
  • Conversion events: such as WhatsApp clicks, form submissions or completed purchases, so you measure real results, not just visits.

7. Testing and launch

Before going live, I test the site on real phones and devices of different sizes and across several browsers. I check that every button works, every form arrives, pages load fast and the copy is error-free.

Then we launch, with the domain and hosting registered in your name.

Tip: Your domain and hosting should be in your name and under your control. That way the site is truly yours and not tied to anyone else.

8. After launch: the real work begins

A website isn't a project that ends on delivery day. To stay effective it needs:

  • Updates: new prices, new services, seasonal offers.
  • Ongoing content: articles and pages that answer your customers' questions and strengthen your Google presence.
  • Backups and security: so if anything goes wrong, the site is back up quickly.
  • Data-driven improvements: we look at the numbers, see where people drop off and adjust to get better results.

What a well-built website gives your business

  • Credibility around the clock: your site introduces you and answers questions even while you sleep.
  • An audience you own: social pages can be restricted or lose reach, but your website is yours and no one can take it away.
  • Better ad results: a fast page that matches the ad keeps visitors moving forward, which often lowers your cost per result.
  • Visibility on Google: when someone searches for your service, they find you, not just your competitors.
  • Leads: a form or WhatsApp button turns a visitor into someone you can follow up with.
  • Online sales: if you sell products, you can take orders at any hour.
  • Decisions based on data: instead of guessing, you know what's working and what needs to change.

The bottom line

A professional website isn't just a nice look; it's a complete business tool that starts with understanding your business and keeps working after launch.
