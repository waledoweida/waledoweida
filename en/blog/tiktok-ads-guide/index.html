TikTok ads that sell: how we run them

By Waled Abu Oweida
October 11, 2026 · 6 min read

"I tried TikTok ads, spent money and got zero orders." I hear this a lot, and the ad is usually a TV-style video or a static image crammed with text. TikTok has its own language, and businesses that speak it sell very well there. Here's how we run TikTok ads that bring in sales, not just views.

Who's on TikTok, and does your business fit?

TikTok isn't just for teenagers. Young adults, parents and business owners across Egypt, Libya and the Gulf spend hours watching. Businesses do well when their product can be shown in a video:

  • Fashion, accessories, perfume and beauty products.
  • Restaurants, cafés and dessert shops.
  • Products with a clear before-and-after.
  • Courses and services where the owner can explain on camera.
  • Online stores with a clear product at an accessible price.

When is it the wrong channel? If you sell a complex service to large companies with long buying cycles, if your audience simply isn't there, or if you can't produce videos consistently. Then Google, LinkedIn or Facebook may fit better, and I'll tell you so upfront.

The essentials: Ads Manager and the Pixel

Everything starts in TikTok Ads Manager. But before spending anything, install the TikTok Pixel on your site: a small code that tells TikTok who visited, added to cart and bought.

Alongside it we set up the Events API, which sends the same events from the server, so data stays accurate even when browsers block cookies. Better data means the algorithm learns faster who actually buys.

Tip: Test the Pixel before launch and make sure your purchase or lead event fires correctly. A campaign without proper tracking is like driving at night with the headlights off.

Choose the right campaign objective

Each objective sends the algorithm looking for a different kind of person:

  • Reach: as many people as possible, useful for a launch.
  • Traffic: visits to your website or a specific page.
  • Video Views: builds an audience to retarget later.
  • Community Interaction: followers and engagement on your profile.
  • Lead Generation: an instant form inside TikTok.
  • Website Conversions: orders on your site, the key objective for sales.
  • App Promotion: installs and in-app activity.

If you want sales, don't pick Video Views. You'll get people who love to watch, not people who buy.

The ad formats we use

  1. In-Feed Ads: your ad appears between videos on the For You feed. The core format.
  2. Spark Ads: you boost a video already posted on your account, or a creator's with their permission. It still looks like a normal post, and engagement adds up on the original, which builds trust.
  3. TopView: the first thing users see when they open the app. It needs a big budget and suits large brands with a major launch.

Smart+ campaigns let TikTok handle targeting, budget and picking the best video. They work well once you have a working Pixel and plenty of creatives, but still need watching.

Creative rules: where everything is decided

On TikTok, the video is the targeting. If it's weak, no setting will save the campaign. Our rules:

  • Make it native, not "ad-looking": the video should feel like part of the app, not an interruption.
  • Hook in the first one or two seconds: a question, a problem or a striking result that stops the scroll.
  • Shoot vertical 9:16: full screen, not a horizontal video with black bars.
  • Sound on: most people watch with sound, so use a clear voice or fitting music.
  • Ride the trends: a popular sound or format makes the video feel familiar, as long as it suits the brand.
  • On-screen captions: for muted viewers, and to reinforce the message.
  • UGC and creators: a real person trying the product persuades more than a studio shoot.
  • A strong offer and a clear CTA: tell people what they get and why now, then exactly what to do: "Order now" or "Message us on WhatsApp".
Tip: Creatives burn out fast on TikTok. Launch fresh ones regularly instead of waiting for results to drop.

Targeting, countries and dialects

Broad targeting often works very well, because the algorithm learns from the video who is interested. You can still use:

  • Interests and behaviours: for niche products, to help early on.
  • Custom Audiences: site visitors, people who watched your videos, or your customer list.
  • Lookalike Audiences: people similar to your customers, once you have enough data.

Separate countries: customers in Libya, Saudi Arabia and Egypt differ in prices, payment and delivery. Dialect matters too: an Egyptian Arabic video may shine in Egypt and land less strongly in the Gulf, so we make market-specific creatives when the budget allows.

Budget, the learning phase and testing

Every new campaign goes through a learning phase, and results swing during it. That's normal. Editing constantly or switching off early sends it back to square one.

  • Set a daily budget big enough for the algorithm to learn.
  • Put several creatives in the same ad group and let TikTok show you which wins.
  • Test one thing at a time: a different hook, a different offer or a different creator.
  • When you find a winning video, scale the budget gradually, not all at once.

The numbers we watch

  • CPM: cost per thousand impressions.
  • CTR: how convincing the video and call to action are.
  • 2-second and 6-second view rates: whether the hook works.
  • CPA: cost per order or lead, which decides profitability.
  • ROAS: revenue for every dollar spent.
  • Comments: questions about price and sizes mean real interest; repeated complaints mean something needs fixing.

Send them to a fast page or WhatsApp

A great ad is wasted if the click lands on a slow site. TikTok visitors have little patience, so you need a fast mobile landing page with the product, the price and a visible order button.

If you sell through conversation, send people to WhatsApp or use a Lead Form. Either way, reply quickly, because interest cools fast.

Mistakes I see all the time

  • Recycling TV ads: they look like ads instantly and get skipped.
  • Only one or two creatives: nothing to choose from, and fast burnout.
  • Killing ads after a day: without giving the learning phase a chance.
  • Ignoring comments: replying builds trust and brings orders.
  • The wrong objective: choosing views when you want sales.
  • A slow or confusing landing page: you pay for the click and lose it in seconds.

The bottom line

TikTok ads sell when the video feels native and grabs attention from the first second, tracking is set up properly, testing never stops, and the customer has an easy way to order. There's no magic formula, just organised work and learning from the numbers.
