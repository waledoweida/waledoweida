Professional Facebook & Instagram ads, step by step

By Waled Abu Oweida
October 11, 2026 · 6 min read

The "Boost post" button is easy and tempting, but serious Facebook and Instagram advertising happens inside Meta Ads Manager. The difference is like casting a net and hoping, versus knowing where the fish are and what you're after. Here's how we build a campaign, step by step, from setting up the account to scaling what works.

1. Before the first dollar: accounts and the Pixel

Every campaign needs a solid foundation:

  • Business Manager: your Page, Instagram account and ad account are registered to your business, and I work on them with granted access, not the other way round. That way everything stays yours.
  • Meta Pixel: a small snippet on your website that tells Meta who visited and what they did: viewed a product, added to cart, purchased, submitted a form.
  • Conversions API: sends the same events straight from the server, recovering some of what the Pixel can miss because of privacy settings and ad blockers. Together they give Meta a clearer picture, and the algorithm learns faster.
Tip: Without a Pixel, Meta doesn't know who bought from you, so it can't find more people like them. Install it even if you're not advertising yet.

2. Choosing the objective: the most important decision

Meta shows your ad to the people most likely to take the action you choose as your objective, not the one you have in mind. The wrong objective means the wrong results:

  • Awareness: get your name in front of as many people as possible at the lowest cost.
  • Traffic: send people to your website or page.
  • Engagement: likes, comments and video views.
  • Leads: a form inside Facebook or on your site.
  • Sales: actual purchases from your store, which needs a working Pixel.
  • Messages: conversations on WhatsApp, Messenger or Instagram Direct. This is one of the best fits for many businesses in Egypt, Libya and the Gulf, where customers like to ask before they buy.

3. The structure: campaign, ad set, ad

Every campaign has three levels:

  1. Campaign: where you pick the objective.
  2. Ad set: audience, budget, placements and schedule.
  3. Ad: the image or video, the copy and the button.

4. Audiences: who sees your ad?

There are several approaches, and we use them depending on the stage:

  • Core targeting: location, age, gender and interests. A service in Tripoli should target Tripoli and the areas it actually serves, not all of Libya.
  • Custom audiences: website visitors, people who engaged with your Page or watched your videos, or your existing customer list.
  • Lookalikes: Meta takes your customers and finds similar people in the same country.
  • Advantage+ broad targeting: you give the algorithm more room to find buyers itself. It works well once the Pixel has enough data and the ad itself makes clear who it's for.

5. Placements and formats

Ads can appear in the Feed, Stories or Reels across Facebook and Instagram. We usually keep automatic placements but prepare creative sized for each. The main formats:

  • Image: quick and simple, good for a clear offer.
  • Video: the strongest for explaining a service and building trust.
  • Carousel: several swipeable cards, great for multiple products or steps.
  • Collection: a store-like experience inside the app, ideal for e-commerce.

6. Creative that stops the scroll

Your ad competes with friends' photos and cat videos:

  • The first 3 seconds: open with the problem, the result or a question that hits home, not with your logo and an intro.
  • A clear hook: an opening line that makes people keep watching.
  • An understandable offer: what do they get, and why now?
  • One clear call to action: message us on WhatsApp, order now, book.
  • Vertical 9:16 video: Stories and Reels fill the whole screen.
  • The target country's dialect: an ad for a customer in Saudi Arabia lands better in a dialect close to theirs than the same ad in Egyptian, and vice versa. Add captions, since many people watch without sound.

7. Budget, the learning phase and testing

When a campaign starts, it enters the learning phase, while the algorithm experiments to find out who responds. Results swing during this period, and that's normal.

  • Don't keep editing: every significant change to audience, budget or creative sends the campaign back into learning. Give it a few days.
  • A realistic budget: a tiny budget on an expensive objective like sales means the campaign learns very slowly. Start with a budget you can sustain through a full test period.
  • A/B test creative: we run several ads with different hooks or formats, then pause the weak ones and keep the winner.

8. Reading the results

These numbers tell you where the campaign is heading:

  • CPM: cost per thousand impressions, showing how expensive it is to reach your audience.
  • CTR: the share of viewers who clicked, a direct read on how strong the creative and message are.
  • CPC: cost per click.
  • Cost per result: the most important number: the cost of each message, lead or order.
  • ROAS: return on ad spend, how much revenue each unit of spend brought back.
  • Frequency: how many times the average person has seen the ad. When it climbs and results start to drop, the audience is tired and needs fresh creative.
Tip: Don't judge a campaign on one number. A high CTR with an expensive cost per result usually means the problem is the page people land on, not the ad.

9. Scaling and retargeting

Once an ad performs at a good cost, we scale gradually: raising the budget step by step instead of doubling it overnight, or duplicating the winner onto a new audience. At the same time we build a full funnel:

  1. Top of funnel: a video introducing your business to new people.
  2. Middle: an ad with more detail for everyone who watched or engaged.
  3. Bottom (retargeting): a clear offer or reminder for people who visited the site or added to cart without buying.

10. Mistakes I see all the time

  • Relying on the Boost button: limited options and far less control than Ads Manager.
  • An audience that's too narrow: stacking interests on a small city makes the ad expensive and saturates it fast.
  • The wrong objective: engagement when you want sales.
  • No Pixel: spending money without learning anything from the people who buy.
  • A weak landing page: a great ad leading to a slow or confusing page, so the customer closes it and leaves.

The bottom line

Successful Facebook and Instagram ads aren't luck. They're the right objective, a well-chosen audience, creative that grabs attention, a page that closes the sale, and close attention to the numbers.
